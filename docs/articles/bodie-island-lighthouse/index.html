Bodie Island Lighthouse: Climbing It, and What It Costs

An 1872 tower on the marsh south of Nags Head, 219 steps to the balcony, and tickets that only go on sale the morning you climb.

Published August 31, 2026 · 6 min read

In short

Bodie Island Lighthouse stands off Highway 12 south of Nags Head, inside Cape Hatteras National Seashore. The grounds and parking are free all year. Climbing costs $10 for adults and $5 for seniors 62 and over and children under 12, and the 2026 season runs April 22 to October 12. There are 219 steps, a 42 inch minimum height, and tickets are sold on Recreation.gov from 7 a.m. on the day of the climb only.

The lighthouse most people photograph on the Outer Banks is Cape Hatteras. The one you can actually climb this year is Bodie Island, twenty minutes south of Nags Head, standing on open marsh with a boardwalk running out behind it.

It is a working tower with a first order Fresnel lens, and the climb is genuinely a climb. 219 steps, no lift, and a balcony at the top with nothing between you and the sound but a railing.

The tower you see now is the third one

Two lighthouses failed here before this one worked. The first, built in 1848 at 54 feet, was put up without a proper foundation and began to lean and sink. The second, finished in 1859 at 80 feet, lasted two years: Confederate forces destroyed it in May 1861 to stop the Union using it.

The current tower was lit on October 1, 1872. It stands 156 feet, carries a first order Fresnel lens, and wears the black and white bands that tell sailors which light they are looking at. It was rehabilitated between 2009 and 2012 and opened to climbers in April 2013, which is recent enough that plenty of locals still remember when you could only look at it.

Climbing it

219 steps to the balcony. The staircase spirals up the inside of the tower with landings along the way, and most reasonably fit people manage it without drama, though you will feel it. There is no lift and no way to skip the stairs.

The rules are firm and the rangers enforce them. You must be at least 42 inches tall and under 260 pounds. Children under 12 need an adult 16 or older with them, and no child may be carried. No bare feet, and no heels taller than an inch and a half. It is worth measuring a keen seven year old at home rather than finding out at the door.

Tickets are a morning job

This is the part that catches people. Climb tickets are sold on Recreation.gov only, only for the same day, and they are released at 7 a.m. that morning. There is no advance booking and no ticket window at the lighthouse.

In July and August the morning slots go quickly. Set an alarm, book from your phone before you get up, and treat the rest of the day as flexible. Climbs run every 20 minutes from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. through the season, which in 2026 is April 22 to October 12. There are also Thursday evening climbs from June 11 to October 1, and a full moon climb each month from May to September, both of which are worth the effort if you can get on one.

If you cannot get a ticket, the climb line is (252) 475-9054.

The keepers' quarters are still shut

The Double Keepers' Quarters, the white building beside the tower that normally holds the visitor center and bookstore, has been closed since an electrical fire on January 7, 2025. Repair work began in early March 2026. No reopening date has been announced.

Plan around it. There is no bookstore, and the restroom situation is not what it was, so use the facilities before you drive down. The building is still worth looking at from outside: it is a handsome piece of 1870s government architecture, and the fire damage is not visible from the lawn.

The marsh is half the reason to come

A boardwalk runs from the parking area out into the marsh behind the station, and it is free, open all year, and takes about ten minutes to walk. It is flat and easy, which makes it the part of the site that works for people who are not climbing anything.

Come early or late and it is one of the better birding spots on this stretch of coast. Herons, egrets and ibis work the shallows, and the light across the grass at either end of the day is better than anything you will get at midday.

How the site changed

The station was automated in 1940, which ended the keepers' era. The photograph below was taken by the Coast Guard four years later, before the parking lot existed, and it shows how isolated the place was: a tower, two buildings, and sand.

Cape Hatteras National Seashore took the light on in 2000, and the National Park Service has run it since.

Practical notes

The grounds, the parking and the boardwalk are free, and there is no entrance fee for Cape Hatteras National Seashore. Only the climb costs money.

Allow an hour if you are climbing, half that for the grounds and the boardwalk. The lot is not large and fills mid-morning in summer. The park office is on (252) 473-2111.

One more thing worth knowing if you are planning a lighthouse trip: Cape Hatteras Lighthouse is closed for climbing and is not expected to open in 2026. Bodie Island is the one to aim at.

Seasons and prices move, so check the official National Park Service page before you drive down.

Common questions

How much does it cost to climb Bodie Island Lighthouse?

$10 for adults, and $5 for seniors 62 and over and children under 12. The grounds, the parking lot and the marsh boardwalk are free. There is no entrance fee for Cape Hatteras National Seashore itself.

How many steps are there?

219 to the balcony, which is roughly a ten story building. The staircase is a spiral with landings on the way up, so you can stop and let your legs recover.

When can you climb it in 2026?

April 22 to October 12. Climbs run every 20 minutes between 9 a.m. and 5 p.m. There are also Thursday evening climbs from June 11 to October 1, and full moon climbs monthly from May to September.

How do you get tickets?

Online at Recreation.gov only, and only on the day of the climb, released at 7 a.m. There is no advance booking. Summer mornings sell out, so set an alarm rather than deciding over breakfast.

Are there height or age restrictions?

Yes. Climbers must be at least 42 inches tall and under 260 pounds. Children under 12 need an adult 16 or older with them. No bare feet, no heels over an inch and a half, and nobody may be carried up.

Staying nearby?

Bodie Island is about 20 minutes south of our places in Kill Devil Hills, and 25 from Kitty Hawk.
