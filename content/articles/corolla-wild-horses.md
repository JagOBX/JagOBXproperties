Most of the Outer Banks wildlife you hear about turns out to be a nature centre
exhibit. This one is not. Loose on the beach and in the woods north of Corolla
is a herd of Banker horses, descended from Colonial Spanish stock, and in June
2010 the North Carolina General Assembly made the Colonial Spanish Mustang the
official horse of the state.

They are not fenced, not fed and not tame. They are also not a photo
opportunity that the law leaves up to your judgement, which is the part most
visitors do not find out until somebody in a county truck explains it to them.

## A managed wild herd, which is a harder job than it sounds

The Corolla Wild Horse Fund manages the herd across 7,544 acres of beach, dune
and maritime forest. That acreage also holds roughly 700 houses, thousands of
people and a steady stream of vehicles, which is the whole problem in one
sentence. This is not a remote refuge. It is a neighbourhood that horses walk
through.

Population is controlled with an immunocontraceptive called PZP, administered
once a year by trained staff using a CO2 pistol, a rifle or a blowgun, with the
training done at the Science and Conservation Center in Montana. It is the least
invasive method available, and it is why the herd stays at a size the habitat
can carry rather than crashing.

There is also a rule that surprises people who bring their own animals: it is
unlawful to keep, ride, walk or bring a domestic horse into the area from the
end of the paved portion of NC 12 up to the Virginia line. The point is to keep
disease and interbreeding away from the wild herd.

![A single wild Banker horse grazing on a grassy dune above the sand near Corolla](/images/corolla-horse-grazing.jpg "The herd spends as much time on the dunes and in the woods as on the sand. Photo by Kabongolei, CC BY-SA 4.0.")

## Getting there, and the rules that carry a penalty

The pavement on NC 12 stops at the north end of Corolla. Past that the beach is
the road.

**You need four wheel drive.** Currituck County's guidance is not hedged: if
your vehicle is not equipped with four wheel drive, do not attempt to drive on
the beach. Air your tyres down before the ramp, around 20 psi for an SUV or a
half ton 4x4 and about 35 for a three quarter ton, and there are air stations at
Historic Corolla Park. Do not stop on the ramp itself. Fill the tank first,
because there are no petrol stations up there at all.

Once you are on the sand the speed limit is 35 mph, dropping to 15 mph within
300 feet of any person. Between mileposts 14.5 and 17, from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.
between the Friday before Memorial Day and Labor Day, vehicles have to use the
lanes next to the dune line. After dark the whole beach, parking areas included,
counts as driving lanes.

To park on the beach you need a county issued beach parking permit, required
from the second Saturday of May through the last Saturday in September. If you
are renting a house in the 4x4 area your rental company or the owner normally
supplies two. If you are staying further south, you can buy a weekly permit from
the county. Sort this out before you drive north.

And then the rule this whole article is named after. **The Currituck County wild
horse ordinance makes it punishable by law to feed a wild horse or to come
within 50 feet of one.** Cruelty, enticing, luring, harbouring, seizing a horse,
and failing to report an injured one are all offences too. Fifty feet is about
four car lengths. If a horse walks toward you, you are the one who has to move.

The reason for the feeding ban is worth stating plainly. These animals live on
marsh grass and salt tolerant plants. Garden vegetables and bread cause colic
that kills them, and a horse that learns people mean food starts approaching
cars. That is how horses die here.

If you would rather not drive it, the Corolla Wild Horse Fund keeps a museum and
gift shop at 1130E Corolla Village Road in Corolla, open Tuesday to Saturday
from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. in season, and the number is (252) 453-8002. Commercial
tour operators run trips up the beach daily in season and take the driving off
your hands.

Rules, permit seasons and prices change, so check
[Currituck County's beach driving page](https://currituckcountync.gov/beach-parking/)
and the [Corolla Wild Horse Fund](https://www.corollawildhorses.com/) before you
go.
