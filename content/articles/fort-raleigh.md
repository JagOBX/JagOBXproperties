The Outer Banks charges you for most things. Fort Raleigh charges you for
nothing, and the car park at the north end of Roanoke Island is usually half
empty while the beach roads are solid.

What happened here is the reason the National Park Service owns it. Between 1584
and 1590 Sir Walter Raleigh's voyages put English settlers on this island. The
last group, 116 men, women and children, including two who were born here, were
gone by the time a relief ship got back. That is the whole of what is known. The
rest is guesswork, and the park is refreshingly straight with visitors about
which is which.

## The earthwork is the actual site

The low turf mound near the visitor center is a reconstruction built from what
the archaeologists found, not a film set. Expect a ditch and a bank about the
height of your shoulder, with a sand path cut through it. It takes five minutes
to walk and it is the oldest thing in this part of the country that you can put
your hand on.

Stand in it for a moment before you move on. This is the ground the whole story
is argued over, and it is smaller than most people's back garden.

![A sand path cut through the low reconstructed earthwork bank at Fort Raleigh](/images/fort-raleigh-earthwork-mound.jpg "The bank and ditch were rebuilt from archaeological records, not imagination. Photo by Sarah Stierch, CC BY 4.0.")

## Two trails, and they are not the same walk

The **Thomas Hariot Nature Trail** is the short one. A 0.3 mile sandy loop
starting beside the earthworks, through maritime forest, with wayside exhibits
on both Algonquian and English history and access paths down to the beach. From
the shore you are looking across Roanoke Sound at Nags Head, Kill Devil Hills
and Kitty Hawk, and on a clear day the Wright Brothers monument is visible on
top of Big Kill Devil Hill.

The **Freedom Trail** is the serious one. It runs 1.25 miles one way from the
Elizabethan Gardens car park through loblolly pine to Freedmen's Point, near the
old Mann's Harbor bridge. After the Battle of Roanoke Island in 1862 a Freedmen's
Colony was established here for people who had been enslaved, and the trail
carries nine silhouettes and eight wayside exhibits, each one about a named
individual from that community. Bikes are not allowed on it.

If you want a loop rather than an out and back, the Roanoke Island Multi-Use
Trail runs along Highway 64 and joins up for a 2.5 mile round trip. Watch for
cyclists and for cars at the Weir Point Drive crossing.

## The practical part, including the bit that catches people out

The address is 1401 National Park Drive, Manteo, and the number is
(252) 473-2111. Grounds are open around the clock. The Lindsay Warren Visitor
Center runs 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. every day and shuts on Christmas Day, so an
afternoon arrival in December is fine and a late one is not.

Here is the thing people get wrong. **Three operations share this entrance and
only one of them is free.** The national historic site costs nothing. The Lost
Colony outdoor drama, run by the Roanoke Island Historical Association, and the
Elizabethan Gardens next door are separate organisations with their own tickets
and their own seasons. Turning up expecting the drama to be included is the
standard mistake.

There is one more thing worth knowing if you have a long drive behind you. The
grounds are wooded and shaded, which in July and August makes this one of the
few outdoor attractions on the Outer Banks where you are not standing in full
sun. Bring water anyway. The trails have none.

The park also has a Junior Ranger programme and ranger talks that move around
the calendar, so if you are travelling with children it is worth checking what
is on before you set off.

Hours and partner ticket prices change, so check the
[official park page](https://www.nps.gov/fora/index.htm) before you drive over.
