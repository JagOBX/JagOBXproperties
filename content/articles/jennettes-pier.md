Most of what there is to do on the Outer Banks costs real money or requires you
to cross a lot of sand. Jennette's Pier does neither. Two dollars gets you a
thousand feet out over the Atlantic on a flat concrete deck, and you can do it
in a wheelchair, in flip flops, or with a pushchair.

The pier you walk on is not the old one. Work began on the original in May 1939,
built by Warren Jennette Sr. of Elizabeth City, and it ran 750 feet on wooden
pilings. Hurricane Isabel took it apart in 2003. What replaced it was dedicated
in May 2011: 1,000 feet of concrete, wind turbines on the deck, and the North
Carolina Aquariums running the whole thing.

## The two dollar walk is the point

Walk-on sightseeing is $2. That is not a typo and it is not a promotion. It buys
you the full length of the pier, which on a rough day means standing directly
above water that is throwing itself at the pilings underneath you.

Go at either end of the day. Sunrise over the ocean from a thousand feet out is
the reason people who live here still pay the two dollars, and the pier opens at
6 a.m. through the summer specifically so you can.

![Waves breaking hard against the concrete pilings under the pier deck](/images/jennettes-pier-pilings.jpg "The concrete pilings are the whole reason this pier survived storms the last one did not. Photo by Roger Mulligan, CC BY 2.0.")

## It makes its own surf break

A pier changes how sand moves, and the sandbar that builds alongside Jennette's
produces one of the more reliable breaks on this part of the coast. On a good
swell there will be surfers working the water on both sides of the structure,
and from the deck you are looking almost straight down at them.

That is worth knowing in both directions. If you are here to surf, this is where
to look. If you are here to swim with small children, pick a stretch of beach
well away from the pier, because rip currents run alongside these structures and
the surfers are there for a reason.

![A surfer riding a wave in the broken water beside the pier](/images/jennettes-pier-surfer.jpg "The sandbar beside the pier is what makes the break. It is also why this is a poor place to swim. Photo by Roger Mulligan, CC BY 2.0.")

## Fishing, hours and the practical part

An all day pass is $15 for adults and $10 for children 12 and under. If you are
after cobia or king mackerel there is a $15 add-on pass. Taxes are added on top.

You do not need to bring anything. The pier house rents rods and sells bait, so
a decision made over breakfast can be a morning on the water by nine. That
matters more than it sounds on a coast where most fishing involves a boat, a
charter fee and a booking made weeks ahead.

Because the state aquariums run it rather than a private operator, there are
educational programmes and exhibits in the pier house, and the building doubles
as an events and banquet space.

The address is 7223 South Virginia Dare Trail, Nags Head, and the number is
(252) 255-1501. About ten minutes south of Kill Devil Hills on the beach road.

Hours move with the season, and this is the part people get wrong in winter.
**May through October it is 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. December through March it closes at
5 p.m.** November and April sit in between at 7 a.m. to 9 p.m. It is shut on
Thanksgiving and Christmas Day.

The pier and the pier house are fully ADA accessible, which is rare here and
worth knowing if anyone in your group cannot manage soft sand.

Prices and hours are set by the aquariums and change, so check the
[official pier page](https://www.ncaquariums.com/jennettes-pier)
before you drive down.
