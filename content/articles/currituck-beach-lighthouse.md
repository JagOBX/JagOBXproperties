Corolla is the far end of the Outer Banks, past the last traffic light, where
the paved road stops and the beach becomes a highway. The lighthouse there is the
one nobody painted.

Bodie Island got black and white bands. Cape Hatteras got the spiral. Currituck
Beach was left as bare brick, roughly a million of them, because the coastline
around it was empty enough that its colour alone told sailors which light they
were looking at.

## Why this tower exists

By the 1870s the East Coast had one dark stretch left: 40 miles of unlit
coastline between Cape Henry in Virginia and Bodie Island in North Carolina.
Congress funded a light to close it in 1873.

It was lit on December 1, 1875, as a fixed white light with a red flash every 90
seconds, and its beam reached 18 nautical miles. The tower stands 162 feet. A
keepers' duplex was finished the following May, and more than two dozen families
lived in it over the working life of the light.

![The unpainted red brick of the tower against a clear blue sky](/images/currituck-brick.jpg "Left unpainted, because the colour alone identified it. Photo by Bonnie Gruenberg, CC BY-SA 3.0.")

## The climb is gentler than the number suggests

220 steps sounds worse than it is. The staircase is cast iron, it spirals up the
inside of the brick shell, and it is broken by nine landings. You are never
climbing more than a couple of dozen steps before there is somewhere to stand.

Compare that with Bodie Island, where 219 steps feel steeper because the rhythm
is different. Families who bounced off one lighthouse often manage this one.

You need your hands free for the handrail, so wear a bag you can put on your back
rather than carrying anything.

![Looking up through the centre of the cast iron spiral staircase](/images/currituck-stairs.jpg "Cast iron, spiralling up inside the brick shell. Photo by rpertiet, CC BY 2.0.")

## The landings are worth stopping at

Each landing has a window set deep into brick that is several feet thick at the
base. They are the best part of the interior, and they are where you will
actually notice the engineering: the wall thins as you rise, which is why the
stairwell gets narrower toward the top.

There is no queue system inside. If someone ahead of you is slow, you wait, and
the landings are where that happens without anyone feeling rushed.

![A tall window set deep into the brick wall of the stairwell, with the iron stairs beside it](/images/currituck-stair-window.jpg "The wall is several feet thick at the base and thins as it rises. Photo by Riokausa, CC BY-SA 4.0.")

## What you see from the top

The gallery runs right around the lantern. On the ocean side you get Corolla's
rooftops and then open Atlantic. Turn the other way and it is Currituck Sound,
which is wide, shallow and usually flatter than the sea.

The lighthouse rule about drones applies inside the compound fence, so leave
yours in the car.

![The rooftops of Corolla and the ocean beyond, seen from the lighthouse gallery](/images/currituck-view-corolla.jpg "Corolla from the gallery. The sound side is the better view on a windy day. Photo by Roger Mulligan, CC BY 2.0.")

## The keepers' house and the compound

The 1876 duplex still stands in the compound, a wooden Victorian building with a
steep roof that looks nothing like the tower beside it. It was built as two
homes, one for the principal keeper and one for the assistant.

The grounds are free and pleasant to walk even if nobody in your group wants to
climb, and the museum shop sits at the base with no admission charge.

![The white timber keepers' duplex with its steep roof, surrounded by trees](/images/currituck-keepers-house.jpg "Built in 1876 as two homes under one roof. Photo by The Woz, CC BY 2.0.")

## Make it a half day, not a stop

The lighthouse sits at the edge of Historic Corolla Park, which also holds
Whalehead, a 1920s art nouveau house on the water, and the Outer Banks Center for
Wildlife Education. Parking is shared and free.

That combination is the argument for driving up. Corolla is a fair way north, and
one lighthouse alone is thin reward for the trip. Three things in one car park is
a morning.

![A large white house with green lawns on the edge of the sound, seen from above](/images/currituck-whalehead.jpg "Whalehead sits in the same park, a short walk from the tower. Photo by Michael Bentley, CC BY 2.0.")

## Practical notes

It is run by Outer Banks Conservationists, a private non-profit, not the National
Park Service. That is why it costs $13 while Bodie Island costs $10, and why
there is no America the Beautiful pass discount here.

The lighthouse office is on (252) 453-4939 and the museum shop on (252) 453-6778.
Take the third or fourth left after Corolla's 11 mile marker, just past the sign
for Historic Corolla Park. It closes for thunder, so on an unsettled afternoon
call before you drive up.

Season dates and prices are set each year, so check the
[official Outer Banks Conservationists page](https://obcinc.org/currituck-beach-lighthouse/plan-your-visit/)
before you go.
